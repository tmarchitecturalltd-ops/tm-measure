Photo tips

Home

Take great room photos

A few small habits make a huge difference

Photos fill the gaps that measurements alone can't cover. Five minutes of prep gets you there.

Lighting

Light the room before you shoot

Even good phone cameras struggle in dim rooms. Two minutes of prep lifts every photo.

  • Turn every light on. Ceiling lights, lamps, even the cooker hood.
  • Open blinds and curtains. But don't shoot directly into a bright window.
  • Avoid mid-day sunbeams. Hot patches of sun blow out the exposure.

Camera angles

Shoot from corners, not the centre

Standing in the middle of a room hides the walls. Standing in the corner shows three of them.

  • Stand in a corner. Point diagonally — four corners covers the full room.
  • Hold the phone level. Tilted phones distort wall heights.
  • Use landscape orientation. Wider frame, captures floor and ceiling lines.
  • Tap to focus. Tap a wall on the screen before pressing the shutter.

What to capture

Include the things we'll need to draw

Photos fill the gaps the measurements can't show. The more context, the fewer follow-ups.

  • All four walls in full. Floor to ceiling, corner to corner.
  • Sockets, switches, radiators. Help us position furniture and confirm layouts.
  • Doors and windows. Closed and open, so we can see swing direction.
  • Ceiling features. Beams, downstand, sloping or vaulted sections.
  • Awkward bits. Niches, columns, alcoves, boxed-in pipes, step-ups.

Avoid

Common photo mistakes

These cost us the most time and force us to ask for re-shoots.

  • Blurry shots. Brace your phone against a doorframe in dark rooms.
  • Cropped features. Half a window, the top of a radiator missing.
  • Backlit silhouettes. Shooting into a bright window with the room dark.
  • Photos through doorways. Step in — you lose the wall behind you otherwise.
